## 2. Conventions, binding on every table

1. **Primary key.** `id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid()` on all thirty-two. `audit_event` additionally carries `sequence_no bigint generated by default as identity unique` for total ordering.
2. **Timestamps.** `timestamptz`, UTC, `default now()`. `created_at` is never supplied by the application. Wall-clock day boundaries are computed once, in a site timezone, and only where a rule requires it.
3. **Numbers.** `numeric(p,s)` for every quantity a rule reads or a document prints. No `float`, no `double precision`, anywhere. SI units only: mass in kg, energy in Wh, volume in m³. Rule payloads carry their own units; conversion lives in `src/domain/units.ts`.
4. **Text.** `text`, never `varchar(n)`.
5. **Codes.** `text`, validated in `src/domain` against `TAXONOMY.md`. **No Postgres `enum` types and no CHECK value lists** — adding a category must never require a migration.
6. **Tenancy.** `organization_id uuid not null references organization(id) on delete restrict` on every tenant-scoped table. Platform tables carry none. `catalog_entry.organization_id` is nullable: `null` means a platform-owned global entry.
7. **Foreign keys.** `on delete restrict` by default, stated per row only where it differs. Every FK gets an index. Compliance records are not deleted; retirement is a status change plus an `audit_event`.
8. **Row-level security.** `enable row level security` **and** `force row level security` on all thirty-two. Policies per `TECHNICAL_SPEC.md` §9.5.
9. **Audit.** Every tenant table carries the `after insert or update` trigger `app.write_audit_event()`. Coverage is structural, not a builder's checklist item.
10. **Append-only tables** — marked **APPEND-ONLY** in their heading — have no UPDATE or DELETE policy, have `update` and `delete` revoked from `authenticated`, and carry a `before update or delete` trigger raising unconditionally so the service-role key cannot mutate them either. Corrections are new rows carrying `supersedes_*_id`.
11. **Reserved word.** The table is created as `public."user"` and quoted at every reference. It is **not** renamed to `users`.
12. **`updated_at`** is maintained by a `before update` trigger, never by the application.
13. **Actor columns.** `created_by`, `updated_by`, `*_by` are `uuid references "user"(id) on delete restrict`, nullable only where the actor can legitimately be the system.
14. **Status columns cite their `TAXONOMY.md` system by ID, or state that none governs them.** A `status` column with neither is a defect — it is how one system's value set gets pasted onto another table.
15. **Retired taxonomy values are never coerced by a migration.** `TAXONOMY.md` §6 retired five values at v1.2 — T-26 `expired` (renamed to `overdue`) and T-23's four flat values (`light_category`, `fully_regulated`, `damaged_defective`, `quarantine_hold`, replaced by the six-value composite). All five were retired pre-launch with no production row behind them, and **`Backfill required` is `no` for every one.** The rule for any migration that ever encounters one: **render as-is, do not coerce, do not exclude from counts.** In particular a migration must never infer a classification outcome from retired `damaged_defective` or `quarantine_hold`, because those values carried no outcome dimension — **guessing one fabricates a compliance record**, which is worse than leaving the row unreadable. Backfilling is a separately approved migration recorded in the deprecation log, never an implicit consequence of a retirement.

### 3.4 `tos_acceptance` — **APPEND-ONLY**

The record that the training-rights grant was in force before the first battery was logged. Decision D-2 makes this non-negotiable and day-one; `BUSINESS_RULES.md` §7 governs it. A trigger on `intake_session` insert refuses when the organization has no acceptance at `status = 'in_force'` or `'grace'` (Rules 7.1, 7.2, 7.14).

> **`status` is T-47, not T-42.** T-42 is `rule_version.status` and its `draft` value has no meaning here. The two systems are not interchangeable and the mistake is expensive on this table: **Rule 7.6** stamps training eligibility at the instant of capture and **Rules 7.7 and 7.17** make it irreversible, so a battery logged while this column holds a value that does not exist in T-47 has an eligibility that no later acceptance, backdating or administrative action can recover.

> **This system records organisational consent. It never governs whether an already-captured record may be used for training** — that is **T-12** on `intake_photo`, stamped once and immutable. The T-47 values `lapsed` and `revoked` change what happens next; they never change what already happened (**Rules 7.6, 7.18**).

**Constraints:** `unique(organization_id, document_key, document_version)` — one row per accepted version per organization (T-47); `check (status = 'not_accepted' or (user_id is not null and accepted_at is not null))`.

**Append-only, with one narrow exception.** A consent row must move `in_force` → `grace` → `lapsed`/`superseded`/`revoked` over its life, so **`status` and the revocation columns are the only updatable fields**. A `before update` trigger raises if `document_version`, `document_content_hash`, `training_rights_granted`, `grants`, `user_id`, `accepted_at`, `ip_address` or `organization_id` differ from their stored values. The evidentiary content — what was agreed, by whom, when — is frozen exactly as **Rule 7.5** requires; only the lifecycle state moves. `DELETE` is available to no one.

**Indexes:** `unique(organization_id, document_key, document_version)`; partial `unique(organization_id, document_key) where status in ('in_force','grace')` — at most one live acceptance per document per org; `index(organization_id, status)`; `index(user_id)`; partial `index(reacceptance_deadline_on) where status = 'grace'` (the job that lapses them).

**Constraints — these three do the real work:**

- `unique(jurisdiction_rule_id, version_label)`.
- **Exclusion constraint (GiST):** `exclude using gist (jurisdiction_rule_id with =, daterange(effective_on, expires_on, '[)') with &&) where (published_at is not null)`. Two published versions of the same rule cannot overlap in time. Ambiguity about which version was in force on a given date is structurally impossible.
- **Immutability trigger:** `UPDATE` is permitted only while `published_at is null`. Amending a published rule means publishing a new version with a new effective date. History is never rewritten. `DELETE` is never permitted.

**Indexes:** `index(jurisdiction_rule_id, effective_on desc)`, partial `index(jurisdiction_rule_id) where published_at is not null`.

### 4.4 `format_classification` — **APPEND-ONLY**

**The per-jurisdiction, per-rule-version size/format result for one battery.** Stores `TAXONOMY.md` **T-06**, whose values include `medium_format` — the statutory band covering scooter and mobility packs.

> **This is not a column on `battery_record`, and it must never become one.** The same physical battery falls into a different statutory band in one state than in another. That is not an edge case; it is the entire premise of Phase B1b's multi-state obligation engine (Rules 8.1, 8.2). A single `battery_record.format_category` column can only ever hold one state's answer, which makes every other state's answer wrong. `TAXONOMY.md` T-06 states this as a review rejection.

Keyed on `(battery_record, jurisdiction, rule_version)` per `_ANCHORS.md` §3. Carries the same rule-version provenance discipline as `classification_decision` (§7.1): the row records which version produced it, so an audit reproduces the call (Rules 12.15, 12.16).

**The placement constraint — Rule 4.28, enforced not advised.** A `before insert or update` trigger on `battery_record.container_id` reads the record's **active** `classification_decision.waste_classification` (T-13) and its condition — `ddr_flags` (T-30), `damage_assessment.status` (T-46) and `recall_match.status` (T-35) — and admits the record only to a container whose `container_type` is that exact pair. Because every T-23 value carries both dimensions, two guarantees follow mechanically rather than by builder judgement:

- **No container can ever hold two different waste classification outcomes.** A record whose T-13 outcome is `fully_regulated` cannot enter any `light_category_*` container. *(Note the two systems: `fully_regulated` is a live **T-13** value; T-23's identically-spelled flat value was retired at v1.2 and is a different system — see §2.15.)*
- **No container can hold sound material beside DDR material.** A record with any DDR flag can only enter a `*_ddr` container (**Rules 6.17, 6.18**).

A record whose classification is `undetermined` is **not placed at all** — Rule 3.10 blocks the decision and Rule 3.12 blocks every downstream document until the missing input is supplied. A change to either dimension moves the record, which writes `storage_event` rows. `container_type` is effectively terminal once the container holds material: changing it with contents present is unavailable, and the material moves to a new container instead.

### 6.5 `alert`

## 11. Relationships

### 11.1 Containment has exactly one path

```
battery_record ──▶ container ──▶ lot
                       │
                       └───────▶ shipment
```

`battery_record.container_id` → `container.lot_id` → and separately `container.shipment_id`. **`battery_record` carries no `lot_id` and no `shipment_id`; `lot` carries no `shipment_id`.** Lot and shipment membership are derived through the container, always. Selecting a lot on `/shipments/new` is a UI affordance that adds that lot's containers — it is not a second storage path. Two paths would mean two answers to "what is on this shipment," and a legal document cannot have two answers.

### 11.3 Many-to-many

**There are none, and that is deliberate.** Every relationship that could have been modelled as a join table is a single FK on the child instead — shipment contents through `container.shipment_id`, lot contents through `container.lot_id`, document linkage through an exclusive arc of real FKs rather than a polymorphic pair. On a compliance product, a join table is a second place for a record's membership to be true, and reconciling two answers under audit is not a position worth being in.

## 12. Cross-cutting guarantees this schema encodes

1. **Tenant isolation is a database fact.** 27 of the 32 tables carry an `organization_id` column; `organization` is tenant-scoped by its own `id`, making 28 tenant-scoped in all. The four that are not are `"user"` and the three platform rule tables. RLS is enabled and **forced** on all thirty-two; `TECHNICAL_SPEC.md` §9.5 holds the policy matrix.
2. **P5 is read-only in Postgres.** The auditor role appears in no writer set, so no INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE policy admits it. A P5 write is refused by the database, not by a hidden button.
3. **Rules are data.** Every threshold, deadline, citation and fire-code limit is a `rule_version` row with an effective date and a mandatory citation. No published version may overlap another in time. No decision-bearing row exists without a `governing_rule_version_id` and an `evaluation_trace`.
4. **The record is wide from `0005`.** One `battery_record` holds a mobility-scooter pack and a vehicle traction pack. No CHECK constraint assumes a size, a chemistry or a form factor. The medium-format category exists from the first migration. Adding a column for a mobility pack after `0005` is a defect.
5. **Legal documents are immutable and provable.** `document_render` stores bytes, a content hash and the input snapshot that produced them. Reprints serve stored bytes and never re-render. Corrections supersede; nothing is overwritten.
6. **Audit is append-only and unavoidable.** Written by trigger, immutable three ways, carrying before, after, actor, time and rule version. Retention on shipment records is itself rule-sourced, with a delete guard.
